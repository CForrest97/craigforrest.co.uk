# Product Overview

<!-- Last reviewed: 2026-08-11 -->

## Purpose

This site is Craig Forrest's engineering home on the web. It should help visitors quickly understand who Craig is, that he builds things, and how he approaches engineering work.

The site presents Craig as a pragmatic builder. Its strongest evidence is selected work shown alongside the decisions, constraints, trade-offs, and outcomes that shaped it.

## Audience

### Primary audience

Engineering peers who want to understand what Craig builds and how he thinks about the work.

### Secondary audiences

- Recruiters assessing Craig's experience and approach.
- Potential collaborators exploring shared interests or relevant work.

When audience needs compete, the site should favour useful engineering substance over recruitment or promotional conventions.

## User need

Visitors need a concise, credible picture of Craig without assembling it from a conventional CV, a broad project archive, or a high-volume blog. They should be able to find representative work, understand the judgment behind it, and see enough personality and motivation for the site to feel human.

## Desired outcome

After a first visit, a visitor should understand:

- who Craig is;
- that he builds things;
- the kinds of evidence available for exploring his work; and
- that his approach values practical outcomes and considered engineering decisions.

Specific technologies and domains should be demonstrated through projects rather than narrowing the overall positioning.

## Content pillars

### Projects

A curated selection of Craig's strongest work. Projects should demonstrate how he approaches a problem through the decisions, constraints, trade-offs, and outcomes involved. The showcase is evidence of judgment, not a comprehensive archive.

### Thoughts

Concise working notes drawn from Craig's engineering practice: lessons, decisions, and observations grounded in real work. The section is not a general-purpose blog and carries no publishing cadence or commitment.

### Personal context

Light context about Craig's motivations and personality where it helps visitors understand the person behind the work. Engineering remains the focus.

### Contact

Contact information provided as a practical utility. The site should not frame contact as a conversion goal or actively prompt visitors to get in touch.

## Success criteria

Success is qualitative clarity. A visitor should be able to explain, after a short visit:

- who Craig is;
- what he builds; and
- what distinguishes his pragmatic, decision-conscious approach.

Traffic, publishing frequency, engagement, and inbound enquiries are not primary measures of success.

## Product principles

- Lead with evidence: use selected work and concrete decisions to establish credibility.
- Prefer substance over promotion: explain the work clearly without turning the site into a marketing funnel or résumé-first experience.
- Stay concise and intentional: publish material because it adds understanding, not to maintain volume or cadence.
- Keep it human: include enough personal context to give the work a recognisable voice without diluting the engineering focus.
- Remain maintainable: favour a focused body of useful content over a comprehensive archive.

## Constraints

- Running costs should be as close to £0/month as possible and must stay under £5/month.
- Use TypeScript throughout.
- Host on Cloudflare and/or AWS; hosting is already set up.
- Use Claude Code and Codex as the primary development tooling.

## Non-goals

- A conventional or résumé-first portfolio.
- A comprehensive archive of every project Craig has worked on.
- A general-purpose blog or coverage of arbitrary topics.
- A publishing schedule or obligation to produce content regularly.
- Audience-growth mechanics, engagement optimisation, or traffic as an end in itself.
- E-commerce, monetisation, lead generation, or other conversion funnels.
- An active invitation for enquiries through the contact feature.

## Launch baseline

The launch must include:

- a projects showcase; and
- contact information.

This overview is ready to guide initiative planning. Feature-level decisions, including the presentation and detailed behaviour of projects, thoughts, personal context, and contact information, belong in their relevant initiatives.
